# 00 – Start Here: Roadmap

*Curated October 2026.*

## What this folder is for

The goal is to be able to talk credibly about almost any technology: **explain** core concepts (e.g. how RAG works), **critique** a tech stack and suggest tailored alternatives, and **get the references** (inside jokes, company lore, people) that come up in conversation. Each guide below is a curated reading list. Every link has a note on why it's worth your time, and each guide ends with practice questions.

## The guides

  - [**01 – AI Engineering**](/guide/01): LLMs, RAG, agents, MCP, evals, AI stacks.
  - [**02 – Cybersecurity**](/guide/02): OWASP, auth/passkeys, crypto, famous incidents, AI security, CTFs.
  - [**03 – Systems, Backend & Cloud**](/guide/03): networking, databases, distributed systems, Kubernetes, system design.
  - [**04 – Frontend, Mobile & Developer Tooling**](/guide/04): React/Next, TypeScript, rendering strategies, mobile, Git, CI/CD.
  - [**05 – Evaluating & Critiquing Tech Stacks**](/guide/05): a 7-question framework, classic essays, case studies, common stacks with alternatives.
  - [**06 – Tech Culture, Companies & Inside Jokes**](/guide/06): a glossary of memes, company stereotypes, AI-era slang, and people to know.
  - [**07 – Staying Current**](/guide/07): news sources, newsletters, podcasts, and a daily/weekly routine.

## Suggested 10-week plan (\~3–4 hrs/week, plus the daily news habit)

  - **Week 0 (today):** set up your news routine from guide 07. Subscribe to 2–3 sources max. Skim guide 06 once so the jokes start landing.
  - **Weeks 1–3, AI Engineering (01):** do the "Start here" items, then the Core list. By the end you should be able to whiteboard RAG and agents.
  - **Weeks 4–5, Systems (03):** the "what happens when you type a URL" walk-through, DNS, the System Design Primer basics, Docker/Kubernetes overviews.
  - **Week 6, Frontend & Tooling (04):** React docs, Patterns.dev rendering patterns, the CI essay.
  - **Weeks 7–8, Security (02):** OWASP Top 10, PortSwigger labs, the incident write-ups. Try picoCTF.
  - **Week 9, Stack critique (05):** read the essays and case studies, then critique 3 real stacks (from StackShare, or companies you know).
  - **Week 10 and ongoing:** go back to the "Deep dive" sections that interested you most. Keep the news routine.

## How to make reading stick

1.  **Explain it out loud:** after each item, give a 60-second explanation as if to a friend. If you can't, re-read.
2.  **Use the checklists:** each guide has a "Concepts you should be able to explain" list. Revisit it weekly.
3.  **Answer the practice questions:** they're modeled on real conversations and interviews.
4.  **Build one tiny thing per area:** e.g. a mini RAG app over your class notes, a Dockerized API, a PortSwigger lab. Hands-on knowledge makes conversation much easier.
5.  **Keep a "terms I heard" note:** when a term or joke comes up that you don't know, jot it down and look it up that evening (guide 06 and Fireship's "in 100 seconds" videos are good first stops).

## How to read a research paper (for the AI and security papers)

Read the abstract, then the figures, then the conclusion. Only then read the introduction and method if it still matters. Find a blog explainer first when one exists (each guide pairs papers with explainers).
